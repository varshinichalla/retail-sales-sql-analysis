# Key Insights — Retail Sales Analysis

1. **Electronics generates the highest total revenue** (₹156,905), narrowly ahead of Clothing (₹155,580) and Beauty (₹143,515). The three categories are fairly balanced, with no single category dominating sales.

2. **Female customers account for a higher share of total spend** — ₹232,840 vs ₹223,160 for male customers, a gap of roughly 4%.

3. **May was the strongest sales month** (₹53,150), while **September was the weakest** (₹23,620) — more than double the difference between the two. This could reflect a seasonal dip worth investigating further with more data.

4. **Five customers tie for the top spend**, each at exactly ₹2,000 (CUST015, CUST065, CUST072, CUST074, CUST089) — suggesting the dataset may cap or round customer totals rather than reflecting organic spending variation.

5. **Beauty has the highest average order value** (₹467.48/order) despite having the *lowest* total revenue of the three categories. This means Beauty customers spend more per visit but purchase less frequently — different from Electronics, which wins on volume rather than per-order spend.

6. **The 46–55 age group drives the most revenue** (₹100,690 across 229 orders), followed closely by 26–35 (₹98,480). Spending is fairly evenly distributed across age brackets, with no single group dominating.

7. **No repeat purchases within the same category were found** across all 1,000 transactions — every customer bought from a given category exactly once. This is likely a property of the synthetic dataset rather than realistic customer behavior, and would be an interesting contrast point if compared against real transactional data.

8. **Cumulative (running total) sales grew steadily through the year**, reaching approximately ₹330,000 by September, with the steepest single-month jump occurring in May.
